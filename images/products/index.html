What the law asks of you

In England and Wales, the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 puts a duty on the responsible person for most non-domestic premises. They must carry out a fire risk assessment, provide suitable firefighting equipment, and keep that equipment in working order. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own laws.

Extinguishers should be serviced once a year by a competent person (BS 5306-3). Read the government guidance for employers, or contact us if you are unsure where you stand.

Monthly check, two minutes per unit

  • It is in its marked place and easy to reach
  • The pin and tamper seal are in place
  • The pressure gauge is in the green
  • The body, hose and nozzle are undamaged
  • The signage above it is visible

Questions we get asked

Which extinguisher do I need for an office?

Most offices keep water or foam extinguishers for paper and furniture fires, and a carbon dioxide extinguisher near computers and other electrical equipment. Your fire risk assessment sets the exact mix. If you do not have one, call us and we can talk it through.

How many extinguishers do I need?

It depends on floor area, layout and what you store. BS 5306-8 covers where to place them, and a common starting point is at least two on each floor. Your fire risk assessment gives the figure for your premises.

Can I use water on an electrical fire?

No. Water-based extinguishers are not for live electrical equipment. Switch the power off if it is safe to do so, and use a carbon dioxide extinguisher.

How often do extinguishers need servicing?

A competent person should service each extinguisher once a year (BS 5306-3). You should also look over each one every month: it is in its place, the pin and seal are intact, the gauge is in the green and there is no damage.
